# Related work

## Problem and comparison criteria

This project considers supplied Hermitian unitary oracles $`R_1,\ldots,R_m`$, with $`R_j^2=I`$, and the autonomous generator

```math
\mathcal L_m=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\mathrm{Ad}_{R_j}-\mathrm{Id}),
\qquad \mathrm{Ad}_{R_j}(\rho)=R_j\rho R_j.
```

The total jump rate is one. The task is to implement $`e^{t\mathcal L_m}`$ as a completely positive, trace-preserving channel, with a diamond-norm guarantee on arbitrary inputs and reference systems. The resource is calls to the supplied oracles. Oracle-independent computation is free in the query model, and the lower bounds allow coherent control and selection of the oracles.

For fixed sufficiently small error and $`t\geq1`$, the worst-case query costs are constant for one supplied reflection, $`\Theta(\sqrt t)`$ for two, and $`\Theta(t)`$ for three. The distinctions relevant to comparison are the access model, the channel output requirement, the restriction to Hermitian involutions, and the fixed number of supplied primitives. That number is neither an invariant Lindblad rank nor a promise of spatial locality. The three-reflection hard family uses a system dimension exponential in $`t`$; it does not prove linear long-time hardness at fixed dimension.

Bibliographic records and version-specific links are in [references.bib](references.bib). The source versions below were checked on 31 October 2026.

## General dissipative no-fast-forwarding and the parity method

**Childs and Li (2017)** prove a linear no-fast-forwarding theorem for sparse Lindbladian simulation in black-box models. Their published Section 7, Theorem 10, uses a directed parity-carrying shift: the jump takes $`\lvert n,j\rangle`$ to $`\lvert n-1,j\oplus s_n\rangle`$ and annihilates the bottom state. This operator is non-Hermitian and nonunitary. The reduction from parity is direct prior groundwork for the lower-bound method used here. [Published article and Section 7](https://www.rintonpress.com/xxqic17/qic-17-1112/0901-0947.pdf); [author version v3, 9 October 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/1611.05543v3).

The present construction uses undirected edge swaps with parity labels. Each colored edge oracle is an involution; backtracking cancels its label. Linear progress comes from radial escape on a branching tree. This changes the restricted generator class while retaining the established parity-reduction strategy. The accompanying universal two-reflection algorithm is a separate part of the classification.

The quantum query lower bound for parity is an application of the polynomial method of **Beals, Buhrman, Cleve, Mosca, and de Wolf**. A bounded-error quantum algorithm for parity of $`N`$ bits requires at least $`N/2`$ bit queries. The query reduction must implement every permitted controlled or selected reflection with a constant number of these bit queries. [Journal citation](https://doi.org/10.1145/502090.502097); [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9802049).

## Hamiltonian twirling and structure-dependent acceleration

**Gao, Ji, and Liu** study Hamiltonian twirling through the Lévy–Khintchine representation. Their results include square-root-time simulation of Gaussian dephasing and other time exponents for stable distributions. Their compound-Poisson lower bound, Theorem D.4 in the author version, accesses a Hermitian $`H`$ through a block encoding and uses jumps such as $`e^{iH}`$, which need not be Hermitian involutions. [Published PRL citation](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/qy4b-gzlq); [author v2, 27 January 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10253v2).

The general random-unitary and twirling viewpoint is inherited mathematics. Here the algorithm reduces words in a group generated by two involutions, rather than sampling a time along one Hamiltonian orbit. A generic unitary-jump lower bound does not establish the restriction to three reflections, and the Gaussian upper bound does not establish the universal two-reflection result. The precise theorem and appendix references in this comparison refer to author v2. The published citation is *Physical Review Letters* **137**, 160405 (16 October 2026); the final typeset proof was not independently compared with that public version.

## General channel-simulation upper bounds

**Borras and Marvian (2026)** develop simulation algorithms based on quantum trajectories. Their constant-total-jump-rate setting, $`\sum_\mu L_\mu^\dagger L_\mu=\Gamma I`$, includes the present family with $`\Gamma=1`$. Section 4, Theorem 6 and Corollary 6.1 provide relevant general simulation results. Their general linear obstruction uses the compound-Poisson setting of Gao, Ji, and Liu; it does not by itself impose involutory jumps. [Published article](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2026-04-13-2063/); [author v2, 8 April 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10425v2).

The ordinary Poisson trajectory expansion therefore supplies the familiar linear upper bound. The two-reflection improvement comes from cancelling adjacent equal letters and sampling the resulting endpoint word. Classical sampling and preprocessing must be accounted for separately before making an end-to-end runtime claim.

**Chen, Gao, Wang, and Zhou (2026)** give a general simulation algorithm with additive dependence on normalized evolution time and the precision term, using a block encoding of the Hamiltonian and a projected unitary encoding of the stacked jump operator. Their Theorem 1.2 and Remark 1.3 give the relevant upper-bound benchmark; their general matching lower bound includes the purely Hamiltonian case. This supplies a general $`O(t)`$ constant-error benchmark but does not determine the restricted reflection threshold. [Author v1, 16 September 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18757v1).

**Wang and Ye (2026)** also report optimal additive time-and-precision query complexity for general Lindblad evolution in the block-encoding model. Their abstract identifies Hamiltonian simulation as the general lower-bound benchmark. This comparison uses that scope statement, rather than a model-specific reading of their full proof. [Primary abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17490).

## Resource-dependent circuit lower bounds

**Ding, Junge, Schleich, and Wu (2025)** study simulation cost relative to a specified accessible unitary set. Their convexified circuit depth, Equation (2.2), minimizes the length of products from that set, with arbitrary classical mixtures. They treat symmetric and unitary noise, and Section 5 extends the framework to environment-assisted constructions with encoding, decoding, and additional compatibility conditions. Thus their scope is broader than system-only trajectory sampling. [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-025-05240-6); [author v2, 8 August 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.15357v2).

A comparison with query complexity must specify which operations are charged and which controls are free. Applying their resource-dependent bounds to an oracle model with arbitrary free oracle-independent operations requires checking the corresponding assumptions and constants. The present parity reduction establishes the lower bound directly in that oracle model. This distinction does not rule out an alternative derivation using their framework.

## Zero explicit Hamiltonian and effective coherent motion

**Shang and França (2026)** show that dissipative dynamics with zero explicit Hamiltonian in a GKSL representation can approximate Hamiltonian evolution. Their construction uses $`F=I-i\delta H`$, giving

```math
\mathcal D_F(\rho)=-i\delta[H,\rho]+\delta^2\mathcal D_H(\rho).
```

Theorems 1 and 2 address the quadratic dissipative-time overhead and its necessity under their uniform-in-time simulation assumptions. The paper also derives an obstruction to superquadratic fast-forwarding for this constructed family. [Author v2, 4 October 2026, Equations (3)–(4) and the discussion following Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18533v2).

The jump $`I-i\delta H`$ is generally non-Hermitian, and its dissipator contains an effective coherent commutator. In the present model every jump is Hermitian, and the generator is self-adjoint in the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product. The tree lower bound uses branching propagation in this self-adjoint class. Merely specifying a zero explicit Hamiltonian would not express that restriction.

## Reversible-chain fast-forwarding has a different output contract

**Apers and Sarlette (2019)** fast-forward a reversible Markov chain through its discriminant matrix $`D`$. Their Theorem 1 prepares a normalized amplitude vector proportional to $`D^t\lvert v\rangle`$; its walk complexity includes an inverse factor $`\|D^t\lvert v\rangle\|^{-1}`$. [Published article](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/28721/28721.pdf); [author version](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.02321).

Preparing this normalized amplitude state is a different task from implementing a physical evolution channel on arbitrary quantum input, or sampling the walk's endpoint probability law. The normalization overhead must also be retained. Reversibility of the classical walk embedded in a reflection construction therefore does not, by itself, provide a sublinear-query simulator for the channel considered here.

## Related unpublished reflection-noise research

**Ruge Lin, *Entropy-Controlled Quantum Simulation* (2026)** is a public, unpublished research repository. The comparison here is pinned to commit [`cee0927307f9a3c364bdd6b7c84ebe7f7dc117be`](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Entropy-Controlled-Quantum-Simulation/tree/cee0927307f9a3c364bdd6b7c84ebe7f7dc117be).

That work already gives the exact one-query autonomous single-reflection channel. It studies the cost of one unknown reflection interspersed with known interventions, and separately establishes $`\Theta(\min\{t,n\})`$ constant-error query complexity for $`n`$ commuting one-spin reflection jumps at total rate one. Thus autonomous Hermitian-jump linear hardness with a growing number of supplied jumps is prior work. [Query-complexity results](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Entropy-Controlled-Quantum-Simulation/blob/cee0927307f9a3c364bdd6b7c84ebe7f7dc117be/science/query_complexity.md); [local-noise results](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Entropy-Controlled-Quantum-Simulation/blob/cee0927307f9a3c364bdd6b7c84ebe7f7dc117be/science/memory_and_local_noise.md).

The present contribution fixes the number of supplied primitives at two or three, gives a universal two-reflection acceleration, and supplies a three-reflection lower bound. The earlier dimension-dependent stability result for Hermitian-jump semigroups is consistent with a hard family whose log dimension grows linearly with time; it is not itself a dimension-independent simulation algorithm. [Stability result](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Entropy-Controlled-Quantum-Simulation/blob/cee0927307f9a3c364bdd6b7c84ebe7f7dc117be/science/channel_stability.md).

## Contribution separated from its ingredients

Poisson trajectory expansions, cancellation of involutions, random walks on free products, and the quantum parity lower bound are established ingredients. The result assembled here is a sharp worst-case channel-query classification by the number of supplied binary-dephasing primitives. Its algorithmic consequence is that universal fast-forwardability survives an arbitrary pair of these primitives, while three can already produce linear query cost. The statement concerns their autonomous sum at fixed total rate, with the access and dimension qualifications stated above.
